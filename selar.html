You are not alone

You know what to do.
Starting is the problem.

You have had a task sitting on your list for three weeks. Not because you don't care. Not because you forgot. But because every time you think about starting, something inside your brain refuses to move. You open it, close it, and open it again. Nothing happens. Then you feel ashamed that nothing happened.

That feeling has a name. It is called the Wall of Awful. And it is one of the most common, and least talked about, parts of living with ADHD.

This book does not tell you to "just start small" or "make a to-do list." It explains why your brain works the way it does, and it gives you tools that are actually built for a brain like yours.

Does this sound like you?

This is what unmanaged
ADHD actually feels like.

  • →You forget things that matter to you, not because they don't matter, but because your brain's filing system works differently.
  • →You can focus for six hours on something interesting, then cannot write one reply to a simple message.
  • →You've been called lazy, irresponsible, or careless by people who just don't understand what's actually happening.
  • →You've tried planners, apps, calendars, alarms, and productivity hacks. None of them stuck longer than a week.
  • →You are exhausted from trying to keep up with a world that was not built for how you think.
None of this is a character flaw. It is your brain operating without the right tools. This book gives you those tools.
What you will learn

Named, explained, and made workable.

01
The Wall of Awful
The emotional weight around a task that makes starting feel impossible even when the task is simple. Once you understand it, you stop calling yourself lazy.
02
The External Brain
Brain Dump, Command Centre, Morning Launch, Evening Shutdown. A system that removes the pressure from your memory and puts it somewhere you can actually use.
03
The Dopamine Menu
A personal toolkit of sounds, movement, novelty, and competition that helps your brain cooperate. Built by you, for how you actually work.
04
Time Blindness
ADHD brains experience time differently. This chapter explains the science without jargon, and gives you tools for navigating a world that runs on schedules.
05
RSD & Emotional First Aid
Rejection Sensitive Dysphoria hits fast and hard. The Emotional First Aid Kit is your protocol for those moments, built when you're calm enough to think.
06
The ADHD Operating Cycle
Eight phases from activation to burnout to rebuild. Knowing which phase you're in tells you exactly what you need right now, not what someone else thinks you need.
07
The Failure Audit
When the system breaks, this is how you rebuild without the shame spiral. Not a lecture, a practical process for getting back to the smallest working piece.
08
Money, Relationships & Identity
Chapters on the Sunday Look (money without shame), repair conversations, medication, hormones, burnout, and who you are when the chaos settles.

About the author

Written from the inside.

Daniel Inimfon
Teacher · Adult with ADHD · Author

Daniel is a teacher who lives with ADHD. He is not a clinician, and this book makes no claim to be a medical resource.

What it is: a practical guide built from real experience, grounded in research, and written for the people who needed to hear "I know exactly what that feels like" before they were ready to try a single tool.

He wrote the book he wished existed when he was first trying to make sense of his own brain.
